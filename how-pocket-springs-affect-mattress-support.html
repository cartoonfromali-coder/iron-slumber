How Pocket Springs Affect Mattress Support

Pocket springs are designed to respond independently to pressure, creating a different support experience from traditional interconnected spring systems. Here is how the construction works and what to consider when choosing a mattress.

What Are Pocket Springs?

Pocket springs are individual springs that are enclosed within separate fabric pockets. Instead of every spring moving as part of one connected unit, each spring can respond more independently when pressure is applied.

This construction allows the mattress to react to different areas of the sleeping surface rather than transferring every movement across the entire mattress.

How Do Pocket Springs Provide Support?

When you lie on a pocket-sprung mattress, the springs beneath areas receiving more pressure compress in response. Other springs can remain less compressed where there is less pressure.

This creates a more targeted response across the mattress. The result can be a supportive feel that follows the body's pressure points rather than behaving like one large spring system.

Key characteristics include:
  • Individual spring movement
  • More localised response to pressure
  • Support across different areas of the mattress
  • Potentially reduced transfer of movement between sleepers

Pocket Springs and Couples

One reason pocket springs are popular with couples is that individual spring movement can help limit how much movement travels across the sleeping surface.

For example, if one sleeper changes position, the springs beneath that area can respond without necessarily causing the same degree of movement across the rest of the mattress.

However, the complete mattress construction still matters. Comfort layers and the overall spring system can influence how much movement is actually felt.

Do More Springs Always Mean Better Support?

Not necessarily. Spring count is only one part of mattress construction.

The way a mattress feels depends on several factors, including spring design, comfort layers, mattress thickness, construction quality and how well the mattress suits the sleeper.

Rather than choosing a mattress based solely on the number of springs, consider the complete construction and whether it provides the type of support you need.

Pocket Springs vs Other Mattress Constructions

Pocket springs

Individual springs can respond independently to pressure, making this construction particularly useful for people who want a responsive support system.

Memory foam

Memory foam responds differently, using foam rather than springs to provide pressure-relieving cushioning and body contouring.

Hybrid constructions

Some mattresses combine springs with foam or other comfort materials. This allows the support characteristics of springs to be paired with additional cushioning.

What Should You Check Before Buying?

When comparing pocket-sprung mattresses, look beyond the spring count and consider the whole mattress.

  • Check the type of spring construction.
  • Consider the comfort layers above the springs.
  • Think about your preferred sleeping position.
  • Consider how the mattress will be used by one or two sleepers.
  • Check the recommended bed base.
  • Make sure the mattress size suits the bed frame and room.

The Bottom Line

Pocket springs can provide a responsive support system because individual springs react to pressure more independently. This can be particularly useful for couples and sleepers who prefer a supportive, responsive feel.

However, spring construction should always be considered alongside the mattress's comfort layers and overall design. The best choice is the one whose complete construction matches your personal comfort and support requirements.
